# Reseller and Hardware Channel Motion

Built from the RealWear channel (industrial assisted-reality hardware, 250+ global partners), this covers what changes when the partner is moving a physical product, not just referring into or integrating with a digital one. Treat this as a first-class motion for hardware and physical-product clients, not a variant of the SaaS referral or technology-partner motions covered elsewhere in this skill.

## What's structurally different about hardware channel motions

A SaaS referral partner sends a warm intro and steps back. A reseller or VAR in a hardware motion is often the buyer's actual point of purchase, holds inventory or at least a purchase order relationship, and frequently touches the customer relationship for the life of the product (support, replacement units, upgrades). That changes the economics, the training requirements, and the conflict risk, all of which need explicit design rather than borrowing SaaS referral assumptions.

## Partner types in a hardware channel

**Distributor.** Buys in volume, holds inventory, sells on to resellers or directly to large accounts. Usually fewer, larger relationships. Margin structure is typically the widest here because the distributor carries inventory risk.

**VAR (value-added reseller).** Sells the hardware bundled with services, integration, or a vertical-specific solution (a systems integrator putting the hardware into a broader industrial workflow, for instance). Margin reflects both the hardware resale and the value the VAR adds on top.

**Reseller.** Sells the product largely as-is, sometimes alongside complementary products, without the deeper integration work a VAR does. Narrower margin than a VAR, higher volume expectation.

**Field service or channel rep network.** Common in industrial and enterprise hardware categories: individual reps, sometimes employed by a distributor or VAR, who need hands-on product training because they're demonstrating or supporting a physical device, not just describing a feature set on a slide.

## Margin and economics

Document the margin or discount structure explicitly, by tier, and hold it consistently within a tier. The most common channel failure in hardware programs isn't a bad margin number, it's an inconsistent one: two resellers at the same tier discovering they got different terms. That breaks trust faster than a genuinely tight margin does.

- Set margin by tier (distributor widest, VAR and reseller narrower, scaled to the value each actually adds), not by whoever negotiated hardest.
- Decide up front how margin interacts with the tiering structure in `partner-tiering-and-portal.md`: does moving from Explorer to Builder tier come with a margin improvement, additional MDF, or purely non-monetary benefits (training, co-marketing support). Be explicit; ambiguity on this point is where channel conflict starts.
- Revisit margin structure on a defined cadence (annually is typical), not reactively every time a partner asks for a better rate.

## Channel conflict rules

Define these before the first conflict, not during it:

- **Direct versus channel.** If the client's own sales team can also sell direct, decide explicitly which accounts are protected for which resellers (by named account, by territory, or by deal registration timestamp) and put it in writing.
- **Reseller versus reseller.** Overlapping territory or overlapping named accounts between two resellers at the same tier is the single most common source of channel partner frustration. A clear territory or account-registration rule, applied consistently, prevents most of this before it starts.
- **Escalation path.** Name who resolves a conflict when the rules above don't cleanly answer it (a channel manager, not whoever the reseller happens to reach first).

## Training and certification for field reps

Because a hardware reseller's reps are often demonstrating or supporting a physical device, not just describing it, training needs to cover more than a SaaS partner's product overview:

- Hands-on product demonstration training, ideally with real units, not just a deck walkthrough.
- Basic troubleshooting so a field rep can handle the common first-line questions without escalating every issue back to the client.
- Safety, compliance, or industry-specific certification if the hardware operates in a regulated or safety-sensitive environment (common in industrial and field-service categories).
- A recertification cadence when the product changes meaningfully (a new hardware revision, a significant firmware or software update), so certification doesn't quietly go stale while still counting toward tier criteria.

Tie certification completion into the tiering structure the same way it's tied in for any other partner type: a Builder or Innovator tier reseller should have a materially higher certification completion rate among their reps than an Explorer tier reseller, and that should be checked, not assumed.

## Logistics and inventory touchpoints

These rarely show up in a SaaS partner program and are easy to miss when adapting SaaS-style frameworks to a hardware client:

- Lead time and minimum order quantities, especially for distributors carrying inventory.
- Return and warranty handling: who owns the customer relationship when a unit fails, and how that's routed.
- Demo unit programs: many hardware sales cycles run on loaner or demo units, which need their own tracking (who has one, for how long, in what condition) separate from the general sales process.

## Applying the 1-1-1-1 filter to a reseller motion

The filter still applies, adjusted for the motion:

- **One buyer:** which specific procurement-led segment does this reseller tier actually reach (a named vertical, a named geography, a named account size band).
- **One offer:** the specific margin and support package for this tier, not a negotiated-per-partner blend.
- **One motion:** reseller, distinct from any parallel referral or technology-partner motion the client might also be running; don't let a hardware client's reseller program quietly absorb SaaS-style referral partners into the same tier structure, since the economics don't transfer.
- **One number:** unit volume or resold revenue by tier, tracked separately from any direct-channel number, so the reseller motion's actual contribution is visible rather than blended into total company revenue.
