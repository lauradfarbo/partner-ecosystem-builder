# Benchmarks Reference

Use these sparingly, as context in a proposal or diagnostic conversation, not as the basis for a client's own targets. A client's actual numbers, once tracked, should always override an industry figure. If the client has no tracking yet, that gap is a bigger finding than any number below (see `audit-and-blueprint-framework.md`).

## Widely cited, easily verifiable figures

- HubSpot reported 45% of its revenue coming from partnerships (HubSpot, 2022 annual report).
- Salesforce has reported that roughly 70% of its implementations are partner-led, and that its partner ecosystem generates several times its own direct revenue (commonly cited as an approximate 4-to-1 ratio, originally from IDC analysis; treat the exact multiplier as directionally useful, not precise).
- Shopify's app and partner ecosystem has been cited as driving a meaningful share of new merchant growth, alongside over $1B in partner-attributed revenue in recent annual disclosures.

## Directional research findings (industry survey and vendor data, not audited financials)

- Partner-involved deals have been shown in ecosystem-platform research (Crossbeam's published analyses) to close at meaningfully higher win rates than deals with no partner involvement, with the uplift reported as scaling up with the size and maturity of a company's partner ecosystem rather than staying flat.
- Multiple industry sources (partner platform vendors, B2B SaaS research firms) report partner and referral-sourced customer acquisition cost coming in well below blended average B2B SaaS CAC, often cited in the range of several times cheaper. Treat this as a directional argument for why partner-sourced pipeline is worth the investment, not as a number to promise a specific client.

## How to use these

Lead with the client's own data whenever it exists, even if it's a smaller and less flattering number than the industry figures above. A specific, real number from the client's own program ("our last three partner-sourced deals closed 20% faster than direct") is worth more in a proposal or audit finding than a borrowed statistic, because it's the client's own evidence and it can't be disputed as unrepresentative of their business.

Use the figures above only to frame the size of the opportunity in an early conversation, before any client-specific data exists yet, and say plainly that they're industry reference points, not a forecast for this specific client.
