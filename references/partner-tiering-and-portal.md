# Partner Tiering and Portal Architecture

This is the Explorer/Builder/Innovator structure, adapted per client from the tiering framework built for Miro's 238+ partner tech ecosystem, paired with the portal architecture built from scratch in MindMatrix at RealWear (70% usage, 50% playbook adoption at scale across 250+ partners).

## Why tier at all

A tier structure exists to answer one question for both sides: what does a partner get, and what do they have to do to get it. Without that, "partner" becomes a single undifferentiated bucket, every partner expects the same support regardless of what they've earned, and the partner team spends its time on the loudest partner instead of the most productive one.

Tiering is not a reward for tenure or a badge for logo counting. A partner who signed up eighteen months ago and has sourced nothing sits in the same tier as a partner who signed up last quarter and already closed two deals, if that's what the criteria say. Tenure is not a criterion.

## The three-tier structure: Explorer, Builder, Innovator

**Explorer.** Entry tier. Every new partner starts here regardless of how promising the relationship looks on day one, because promise isn't performance yet.

- Criteria to enter: signed partner agreement, completed onboarding (product/ICP training), listed in the partner directory or portal.
- What they get: self-serve portal access, standard co-marketing menu (see `comarketing-and-launch-playbooks.md`), standard deal registration, no dedicated partner manager time beyond onboarding.
- What's expected of them: at minimum, activation within 90 days (first registered deal, first co-marketing asset used, or first qualified lead sent, whichever fits the motion).
- Review point: 90 days. A partner with zero activity at 90 days gets a direct conversation, not an automatic tier assumption. Sometimes the fix is enablement, not the partner's effort.

**Builder.** Mid tier. Earned through demonstrated activity, not time served.

- Criteria to enter (typically two of three, adjust thresholds to client's deal size and motion): a minimum number of partner-sourced or partner-influenced opportunities in the prior two quarters (set the number based on the client's actual deal volume, not a borrowed benchmark), a minimum win rate on partner-involved deals, or completed certification/enablement beyond onboarding.
- What they get: named point of contact on the partner team (not necessarily full-time dedicated), access to a deeper enablement layer (solution briefs, sales briefs, see the enablement reference), eligibility for co-selling support on active deals, priority queue for support requests.
- What's expected of them: sustained activity against the criteria above, participation in at least one joint go-to-market motion per quarter (a webinar, a case study, a co-sell play).
- Review point: quarterly. Movement in both directions is real. A Builder that goes quiet for two quarters drops back to Explorer support levels, even if the relationship stays warm.

**Innovator.** Top tier. Small by design, reserved for partners who move real pipeline or real product reach.

- Criteria to enter: material, sustained partner-sourced or partner-influenced revenue contribution, or (for technology/integration partners) material product usage lift attributable to the integration, sustained over multiple quarters, not a single good quarter.
- What they get: the bespoke 20% layer described in the co-marketing reference: custom joint campaigns, executive sponsor relationship, input into roadmap or integration priorities, first access to new launches (see the launch playbook), MDF-style co-marketing budget if the client's economics support it.
- What's expected of them: this is the tier where the partner team invests real hours, so the bar for staying here has to be real. Annual (not quarterly) strategic review with the client's own leadership present, not just the partner manager.
- Review point: annually for tier status, quarterly for activity tracking. Losing Innovator status should be rare and should come with a direct conversation, not a silent demotion.

## Setting the actual thresholds

Do not import someone else's numbers as the client's tier criteria. A tier structure only works if the thresholds reflect the client's real deal size, real sales cycle, and real partner pool size. Before setting numbers:

1. Pull whatever partner activity data exists, even if it's incomplete or lives in spreadsheets instead of a CRM. Incomplete data beats no data.
2. Rank existing or prospective partners by actual contribution (deals sourced, deals influenced, leads sent, integration usage). If ten partners exist and two clearly outperform the rest, the tier line sits between partner two and partner three, not at an arbitrary percentage.
3. Set the Builder and Innovator thresholds slightly above current best performance, not at it, so the tiers pull performance up rather than rubber-stamping the status quo.
4. Revisit thresholds at the first quarterly review. Early thresholds are informed guesses. Treat them that way.

## Portal architecture

The portal is the operational home of the 80% template layer. If a partner has a question the portal can't answer, that's a portal gap, not a reason to schedule a call.

### Structure that held up at scale (MindMatrix build, RealWear)

**Home / dashboard.** Partner's own tier status, current deal registrations, and the two or three things they should do next (not a general resource dump on landing).

**Sales enablement.** Battle cards, solution briefs, pricing/packaging one-pagers, objection handling notes. See `sales-enablement-and-lead-to-cash.md` for asset structure.

**Marketing / co-marketing.** The campaign-in-a-box menu (templated assets, see `comarketing-and-launch-playbooks.md`), brand and logo usage guidelines, co-branded template library.

**Deal registration.** The registration form itself, status of open registrations, protection window countdown, and the attribution rules in plain language (see `sales-enablement-and-lead-to-cash.md`).

**Training and certification.** Onboarding path for Explorer, deeper certification path for Builder and above. Certification completion feeds directly into tier criteria, so this section isn't decorative.

**Resource library.** Product documentation, integration guides (for technology partners), and anything else that would otherwise generate a support ticket.

### Content plan discipline (the 80/20 applied to the portal itself)

Most portal content should be built once and reused across every partner at a given tier. The bespoke layer is small and deliberate:

- Templated (80%): onboarding materials, standard co-marketing assets, standard sales briefs, standard training modules. Build these once, well, and maintain a version log so partners always know they're looking at the current version.
- Bespoke (20%, Innovator tier only): joint campaign assets, co-branded material tailored to a specific partner's audience, anything built around a named joint customer story.

### Usage as a health signal, not a vanity metric

Portal usage rate and playbook adoption rate (the 70% and 50% figures from the RealWear build) are diagnostic signals, not marketing claims to repeat client to client. A high usage rate with no pipeline movement means the content is being read but isn't actionable. A low usage rate with strong pipeline from a handful of partners means the top partners don't need the portal, they need direct attention, and the portal's real job is enabling the long tail. Read the two numbers together, not separately. See `audit-and-blueprint-framework.md` for how this shows up in a program audit.
