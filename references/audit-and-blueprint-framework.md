# Audit Framework and the Blueprint Engagement

This is the diagnostic engine behind the Blueprint offer: a fixed-fee audit-and-repair engagement, typically 5,500-7,500 EUR, for founders whose GTM infrastructure (partner program included) was set up at some point, handed off, and left without ongoing monitoring. The client symptom is rarely "we have no partner program." It's usually "we have one, it hasn't produced anything measurable in a while, and nobody's sure why."

## What the audit is actually diagnosing

Most stalled partner programs fail at one of four layers. The audit's job is to find which layer, because the fix is completely different depending on the answer.

**Layer 1: No real strategy underneath it.** Partners were signed opportunistically, without a clear buyer, offer, motion, or metric attached (see the 1-1-1-1 filter in the main skill file). Symptom: a partner list with no discernible pattern in who's on it or why.

**Layer 2: Strategy exists but enablement doesn't.** Partners were recruited with a plan, but nobody built the portal, the sales briefs, or the onboarding path that would let a partner actually sell or refer without hand-holding. Symptom: a handful of partners who only produce when someone personally walks them through it, and the rest go quiet.

**Layer 3: Enablement exists but tracking doesn't.** Partners are active, assets exist, but nobody can say with confidence what pipeline came from partners versus direct, because there's no deal registration discipline or CRM tagging. Symptom: anecdotal partner wins ("I think that deal came through so-and-so") with no reliable number behind them.

**Layer 4: Everything exists but nobody owns the cadence.** Tiering, portal, and tracking are all technically in place, but no one reviews partner performance on a schedule, so drift goes unnoticed for months. Symptom: a program that looks fine on paper and has quietly stopped producing.

Most stalled programs handed off without monitoring sit at Layer 2 or Layer 3. Layer 1 problems usually mean the program needs to be redesigned, not repaired, which is a different conversation with the client about scope.

## The audit checklist

Work through each section with whatever access exists (CRM, portal admin, prior partner agreements, conversations with the internal team). Note what's missing as clearly as what's present; a gap is a finding, not an obstacle to finishing the audit.

### Strategy and fit
- Is there a documented reason each current partner type was chosen, or were partners added opportunistically.
- Does the partner list map to an identifiable buyer segment, or is it a mix with no common thread.
- Is there a single primary motion (referral, co-sell, reseller, integration), or is the program trying to run several without a dedicated owner for any of them.
- Who owns this program today, and how much of their time does it actually get.

### Tiering and performance
- Do tiers exist. If yes, are the criteria performance-based or tenure/logo-based.
- When was the tier structure last reviewed, and has any partner moved tiers in either direction in the last two quarters.
- Pull the actual list of partners ranked by whatever activity data exists. Does the current tier assignment match the ranking, or has drift set in.

### Portal and enablement
- Does a portal exist. If yes, when was it last updated, and what's the actual usage rate (logins, asset downloads, playbook access) versus how many partners are supposed to be using it.
- Are sales enablement assets (battle cards, solution briefs) current, or do they reference an old product version, pricing, or positioning.
- Is there a defined onboarding path for a new partner, or does onboarding happen ad hoc, one conversation at a time.

### Attribution and tracking
- Is there a deal registration process. If yes, is it actually used, or do partners bypass it because it's slow or unclear.
- Can the client currently produce a number for partner-sourced pipeline and partner-influenced pipeline separately, with any confidence. If not, this is very likely the fix that matters most.
- Are attribution rules (first-in registration, protection windows, sourced versus influenced definitions) documented anywhere a partner could actually find them.

### Cadence and governance
- Is there a recurring partner performance review, and is it actually happening on schedule.
- Does partner performance show up anywhere leadership looks regularly (a dashboard, a recurring report), or does it live only in the partner owner's head.
- When a partner goes quiet, is there a defined trigger for someone to notice and follow up, or does it just happen silently.

### Hardware and reseller-specific checks (when applicable)
- Are channel conflict rules defined (who gets credit when direct sales and a reseller both touch the same account).
- Is margin/discount structure documented and consistent across resellers of the same tier, or negotiated ad hoc per relationship.
- Is there a training or certification requirement for a reseller's field reps, and is it actually being completed. See `reseller-and-hardware-motion.md` for the full motion design.

## From audit to Blueprint deliverable

The audit itself is diagnostic. The Blueprint deliverable is the fix, scoped and fixed-fee. Structure the handoff as:

1. **Findings, by layer.** Which of the four layers above is the primary bottleneck, with the specific evidence from the checklist. Resist the urge to report every gap with equal weight; name the one or two that are actually blocking measurable output.
2. **Prioritized fix list.** Ordered by effort versus impact, not by what's most interesting to build. A missing deal registration process that's blocking attribution usually outranks a portal redesign, even though the portal redesign is more visible.
3. **Fixed scope for the repair.** What gets built or fixed within the engagement, stated plainly, and what's explicitly out of scope (this is where the fixed-fee discipline matters most: scope creep on an audit-and-repair engagement is how a 6,000 EUR project becomes an unpaid ongoing commitment).
4. **Handoff plan.** Since Blueprint work is build-and-hand-off by design, the deliverable needs to include what the client's team does after Laura is no longer in the room: who owns the tier reviews, who owns the portal content updates, what the review cadence is going forward.

## A note on scope discipline during the audit itself

It's common, mid-audit, to find problems well outside partner ecosystem work (a broken CRM, a leadership team with no shared GTM view, a product without clear ICP definition). Flag these plainly in the findings. Do not quietly expand the audit to fix them within the same fixed fee. That's exactly the scope creep the Blueprint offer is structured to avoid.
